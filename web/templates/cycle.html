Phase-by-Phase Guide

Each phase of the business cycle has distinct characteristics, leading indicators, and sector tilts. Transitions don't happen overnight — watch the triggers to anticipate what's coming next.

{# --- TROUGH --- #}

Trough

{% if cycle and cycle.phase == 'trough' %}Current Phase{% endif %}

What's happening

The economy has bottomed out. GDP growth is near zero or negative but stabilising. Unemployment is high but new jobless claims are peaking. Consumer and business confidence is at its lowest. Central banks have typically cut rates aggressively and may be using unconventional policy (QE).

Key indicators

  • Yield curve: Steepening after inversion — short rates falling as central bank cuts
  • VIX: Elevated (25-40+) but may be declining from peak
  • PMI/ISM: Below 50 but turning up
  • Credit spreads: Wide but starting to narrow
  • Earnings: Negative revisions slowing, forward guidance improving

Favoured sectors

Financials Consumer Discretionary Technology Real Estate

Why: Rate-sensitive sectors rally first as markets price in recovery before it shows in economic data. Banks benefit from a steepening yield curve. Beaten-down cyclicals get re-rated.

Transition triggers (Trough → Early Expansion)

  • PMI crosses back above 50
  • Jobless claims begin sustained decline
  • Credit conditions ease — lending picks up
  • Consumer confidence turns positive
  • Stock market sustained rally from lows (often leads by 6-9 months)
{# --- EARLY EXPANSION --- #}

Early Expansion

{% if cycle and cycle.phase == 'early_expansion' %}Current Phase{% endif %}

What's happening

Recovery is underway. GDP growth is accelerating from a low base. Employment is growing, consumers are spending again. Interest rates remain low — monetary policy is still accommodative. Credit is flowing. Corporate earnings are recovering quickly (easy comparisons to the downturn).

Key indicators

  • Yield curve: Steep — short rates low, long rates rising on growth expectations
  • VIX: Declining towards 15-20 range
  • PMI/ISM: Above 50 and rising
  • Unemployment: High but falling
  • Earnings: Strong positive revisions, beats common

Favoured sectors

Financials Consumer Discretionary Real Estate Technology

Why: Banks earn more on the steep yield curve. Consumers with pent-up demand start spending on discretionary goods. Low rates fuel real estate. Tech capex rebounds as businesses invest in growth again.

Transition triggers (Early → Mid Expansion)

  • Unemployment drops below long-run average
  • Central bank signals first rate hike
  • Capacity utilisation rises above 75-80%
  • Wage growth accelerates
  • Business investment (capex) broadens beyond recovery sectors
{# --- MID EXPANSION --- #}

Mid Expansion

{% if cycle and cycle.phase == 'mid_expansion' %}Current Phase{% endif %}

What's happening

The economy is in full swing. Growth is broad-based. Employment is strong, wages rising. Central banks are normalising rates — moving from accommodative to neutral. Corporate profits are healthy. This is often the longest phase and the "sweet spot" for equities.

Key indicators

  • Yield curve: Flattening gradually as central bank hikes short rates
  • VIX: Low and stable (12-16)
  • PMI/ISM: Solidly above 50 (52-58)
  • Unemployment: Low and still falling
  • Inflation: Rising but still within target range

Favoured sectors

Technology Industrials Materials Communication Services

Why: Broad growth lifts industrials and materials (construction, infrastructure, commodities). Tech benefits from continued capex and innovation spending. Earnings growth favours growth stocks over value.

Transition triggers (Mid → Late Expansion)

  • Inflation exceeds central bank target persistently
  • Yield curve flattens significantly (10Y-2Y spread < 0.5%)
  • Central bank turns hawkish — accelerated tightening
  • Labour market overheats — wage-price spiral concerns
  • Corporate margins begin to compress
{# --- LATE EXPANSION --- #}

Late Expansion

{% if cycle and cycle.phase == 'late_expansion' %}Current Phase{% endif %}

What's happening

Growth is mature and decelerating. Inflation is elevated, forcing aggressive central bank tightening. The yield curve is flat or inverting. Corporate costs are rising (wages, materials, interest). Profit margins are under pressure. This is the danger zone — the economy is vulnerable to shocks.

Key indicators

  • Yield curve: Flat to inverted (10Y-2Y near zero or negative)
  • VIX: Rising from low levels (16-22)
  • PMI/ISM: Still above 50 but declining
  • Inflation: Above target, sticky
  • Credit: Lending standards tightening, spreads widening

Favoured sectors

Energy Materials Healthcare Industrials

Why: Commodities benefit from inflation and supply constraints. Energy companies have pricing power. Healthcare is defensive with steady demand. Value stocks outperform growth as rising rates compress multiples.

Transition triggers (Late Expansion → Contraction)

  • Yield curve deeply inverts (historically leads recession by 12-18 months)
  • PMI drops below 50
  • Employment growth stalls, jobless claims rise
  • Consumer confidence drops sharply
  • Credit event or financial stress (bank failures, corporate defaults)
{# --- CONTRACTION --- #}

Contraction

{% if cycle and cycle.phase == 'contraction' %}Current Phase{% endif %}

What's happening

The economy is shrinking. GDP is declining, unemployment rising. Corporate earnings are falling. Central banks pivot to cutting rates but it takes time to feed through. Risk appetite is low — investors flee to safety. Cash, bonds, and defensive equities outperform. This is where most investors lose money, but also where the best long-term buying opportunities emerge.

Key indicators

  • Yield curve: Un-inverting as central bank cuts aggressively — but initially still flat
  • VIX: High (25-45+), spikes on bad news
  • PMI/ISM: Below 50 and falling
  • Unemployment: Rising rapidly
  • Earnings: Negative growth, downgrades across sectors

Favoured sectors

Utilities Consumer Staples Healthcare Gold Miners

Why: Defensive sectors with inelastic demand hold up best. People still pay power bills, buy groceries, and need healthcare. Gold acts as a safe haven. Dividends become the main source of returns.

Transition triggers (Contraction → Trough)

  • Rate cuts reach "accommodative" territory
  • Jobless claims peak and begin declining
  • PMI stabilises near 45-48 and stops falling
  • Government fiscal stimulus takes effect
  • Inventories fully destocked — restocking cycle begins
{# ===== HOW DETECTION WORKS ===== #}

How We Detect the Current Phase

The cycle indicator on the dashboard uses a composite scoring model based on three real-time market signals. No single indicator is reliable alone — the combination provides a more robust estimate.

Yield Curve (10Y-2Y Spread)

Inverted < 0% Flat 0-0.5% Normal 0.5-1.5% Steep > 1.5%

The yield curve is the single best recession predictor. When short-term rates exceed long-term rates (inversion), it signals markets expect rate cuts ahead — typically because they expect an economic downturn. Every US recession since 1970 was preceded by an inversion, though timing varies (6-24 months lead).

Scoring: Deeply inverted (<-0.5%) = -3 | Inverted = -1 | Steep (>1.5%) = +2 | Normal = +1

VIX (Volatility Index)

Low < 14 Normal 14-20 Elevated 20-30 Fear > 30

The VIX measures expected S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days. High VIX means fear and uncertainty — correlated with contractions and troughs. Very low VIX suggests complacency, typical of mid-expansion. It's a coincident indicator (tells you where you are now, not where you're heading).

Scoring: Fear (>30) = -2 | Elevated = -1 | Complacent (<14) = +2 | Normal = +1

S&P 500 vs 200-Day Moving Average

Well below < -10% Below -10 to 0% Above 0-10% Well above > 10%

The 200-day moving average is a widely followed trend indicator. When the market is well above it, the trend is bullish and the economy is likely expanding. When below, the trend has broken down. Markets lead the economy by 6-9 months, so this often turns before GDP data confirms a recession or recovery.

Scoring: Well above (>10%) = +2 | Above = +1 | Below = -1 | Well below = -2

{# ===== LIMITATIONS ===== #}

Limitations to keep in mind

  • This is a heuristic model using three market signals — it's a learning aid, not a trading signal
  • Cycle phases don't have clean boundaries — transitions are gradual and messy
  • Sector rotation is a tendency, not a guarantee — individual stocks can buck the trend
  • External shocks (pandemics, wars, policy surprises) can abruptly change the cycle
  • The model uses US market signals — the ASX generally follows the US cycle with a lag, but Australia's commodity-heavy economy has its own dynamics
  • Past sector rotation patterns may not repeat exactly in future cycles
